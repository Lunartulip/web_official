After NVIDIA’s leadership transition, CoreWeave’s expansion depends on funding execution

Following NVIDIA’s change of CEO, compute operators that have relied on its equipment and financial support need to reassess the funding conditions for their next phase of expansion. Our base case is that projects supported by existing customer contracts and committed funding continue to deliver capacity. Additional capacity will enter service on schedule only if customer prepayments and project financing cover the required construction spending. For CoreWeave (CRWV) and its hosting provider Core Scientific (CORZ), the same compute investment creates different payment obligations, so revenue growth translates differently into cash available to equity.

We therefore place greater valuation weight on businesses that are already billing, have funding in place and have a clear path to cash collection. Long-term orders and planned capacity still have value, but delivery conditions, financing costs and senior claims need to be assessed separately. Contracts support growth at both companies; cash recovery is the next test of its quality.

1. Existing NVIDIA contracts continue to support CoreWeave

On November 12, NVIDIA announced Jensen Huang’s departure as CEO and from his other roles, with Colette Kress appointed interim CEO. The board said it expected customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue. As of the research date, NVIDIA had announced no withdrawal of its existing contractual support for CoreWeave. [1]

The capacity-purchase agreement signed in 2025 had an initial value of $6.3 billion and runs through April 2032. NVIDIA is obliged to purchase eligible unsold CoreWeave capacity, subject to delivery and service-availability conditions. The agreement also retains termination provisions, including for breach. This obligation supports demand for specified capacity; it does not extend to all CoreWeave debt or all contracted revenue. [2]

The leadership change introduces uncertainty over future capital allocation, while existing obligations remain subject to their contractual terms. For new projects whose funding is not yet secured, NVIDIA’s willingness to provide additional support will affect financing conditions. A leadership change alone does not establish that support under existing contracts has been withdrawn.

2. CoreWeave needs funding receipts to match equipment payments

In the nine months ended September 30, 2026, CoreWeave generated $5.638 billion of operating cash flow and spent $23.558 billion on cash purchases of property, equipment and capitalized internal-use software. Operating cash flow covered 23.9% of those purchases. The difference was $17.920 billion, while net financing cash inflow over the same period was $21.346 billion. [3]

These figures establish the role of external funding in the expansion. The $17.920 billion is a historical difference between operating cash generation and investment spending, not a current unfunded shortfall. Operating cash flow also includes customer prepayments and other working-capital movements, so it should not be treated entirely as recurring operating earnings.

Third-quarter operating income was $207 million, against net interest expense of $846 million. Revenue growth had yet to translate into net profit. As equipment enters service, the ability of incremental revenue to absorb depreciation and financing costs will determine the earnings attributable to equity. [4]

On the November 10 earnings call, management said that, at then-current yields, it did not intend to issue additional parent-level unsecured debt through the end of 2027. It instead outlined a funding mix of customer-contract-backed project financing, prepayments and other capital sources. The company expected approximately $9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits over the following three months, including more than $3 billion from renewals on existing equipment. Its next delayed-draw term loan was expected to cover more than 90% of the associated capital expenditure. [5]

Those funding plans were disclosed before NVIDIA’s leadership announcement. Together with capacity already in operation, they support continued delivery as our base case, although customer receipts and financing closings still require verification. For additional projects, the pace of deployment will depend on whether customer contracts can support financing and whether funding receipts align with equipment payments. The aggregate order book cannot establish either condition.

3. Core Scientific’s billing progress improves revenue visibility

As of November 5, approximately 485 MW of Core Scientific’s roughly 590 MW CoreWeave contract book was billing, or about 82.2%. Approximately 105 MW was not yet billing. These proportions apply only to that group of contracts; they do not establish whether CoreWeave has begun billing its own end customers. [6]

The relevant leases have fixed fees, take-or-pay terms and no unilateral termination-for-convenience right. These provisions support revenue visibility for delivered capacity, while the arrangements for recovering construction funding affect current cash receipts. CoreWeave funded some construction costs up front and recovers them through credits against eligible monthly fees, capped at 50% of those fees.

For a lease using the full credit allowance, each $100 of eligible monthly fees generates $50 of current cash receipts. Holding the fee constant, receipts increase to $100 after the credit balance is exhausted. This example excludes power pass-through and measures contractual receipts; it is not a conversion estimate for GAAP revenue or free cash flow to equity. [6]

Over the longer contractual horizon, the end of those credits can also trigger principal repayments. The $3.3 billion secured notes issued by Core Scientific Finance I LLC begin amortizing on the first qualifying semiannual payment date at least 15 days after both final commencement under all relevant leases and exhaustion of all applicable revenue credits. Initial annual principal amortization is 11.5% of original principal, or $379.5 million, in addition to interest. The November 2029 start used in the quarterly debt-maturity schedule is an assumption; the contractual conditions determine the actual date. [7][8]

Credit exhaustion increases cash rent, while debt service, reserves and restrictions on project cash accounts continue to govern its use. Allocating the entire increase in collections to equity would overstate the cash released when the credits end.

4. The two companies require different cash-flow assumptions

CoreWeave has a basis for continued revenue growth, but new capacity requires customer payments and external funding to arrive together. Financing costs and delivery delays will affect earnings and equity value. Core Scientific’s already-billing business has less remaining delivery risk, while construction credits and the order of debt repayment determine the cash available to equity.

Our thesis for the next two quarters is that financing execution, delivery and collection will drive differentiation across the chain. We place greater valuation weight on verifiable cash flows and use more conservative realization and cost-of-capital assumptions for capacity still dependent on future financing. Already-billing hosting operations and new compute deployments require separate estimates of revenue timing and cash recovery. Security selection must also account for the purchase price.

5. Which disclosures would change the thesis?

Material changes to NVIDIA’s existing contracts would require us to reassess the protection assigned to the affected projects. If CoreWeave’s customer receipts fall short of expectations, or project-financing terms are less favorable than management anticipates, and alternative funding cannot fill the gap, we would reduce deployment and cash-recovery expectations for the corresponding projects. Conversely, timely funding, continued capacity delivery and limited additional parent capital would reduce our assessment of the financing constraint on growth.

For Core Scientific, the relevant checks are billing capacity, remaining construction credits, the actual amortization trigger and cash available for distribution. Each update should be tied to a specific project’s payment obligation, delivery date or priority claim on cash.

6. How AlphaMap supports the investment judgment

This case combines AlphaMap’s existing equipment, customer and hosting relationships with filings and contracts checked by research agents. The resulting evidence graph places capacity purchases, construction credits and debt payments in the same economic chain, retaining the entities, terms and triggers that govern each obligation.

Those distinctions change the financial analysis. NVIDIA’s capacity purchase protects only specified exposures. Changes in CoreWeave’s financing must pass through lease and collection terms before they affect already-billing hosting operations. Higher hosting revenue must then be assessed against construction credits and debt service before it can support cash available to equity. The case graph connects each judgment to its source and financial assumptions, allowing subsequent disclosures to be applied to the specific model inputs they change.
